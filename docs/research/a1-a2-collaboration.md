# A1/A2 theatre workflow and collaboration research

**Status:** Research snapshot

**Last reviewed:** 2026-09-18

## Role evidence

Theatre job boundaries vary by production, so A1 and A2 must be defaults rather
than rigid identities. A current theatre production handbook describes the A1
as operating the audio/sound-effects equipment, potentially mixing the console,
supervising the audio crew, and performing system and microphone checks. The
same handbook describes the A2 microphone technician as responsible for the
wireless microphone system during the show, coordinating wireless changes with
stage management, performing microphone checks, and ensuring correct performer
placement/use.

Source: [UCF Production Handbook 2025, A1 and A2 position responsibilities](https://cah.ucf.edu/performingarts/wp-content/uploads/sites/46/2025/08/Production-Handbook-2025.pdf)

The UK National Occupational Standard for theatre/live sound also emphasizes
following scripts and cue sheets, understanding Standby/Go terminology, making
consistent cue responses, and ensuring the mix remains free from defects.

Source: [Operate sound for theatre and live productions](https://www.ukstandards.org.uk/en/nos-finder/SKSTTL26/operate-sound-for-theatre-and-live-productions)

## Monitoring and collaboration evidence

Shure's current WAVETOOL 4 material describes up to eight simultaneous users,
real-time channel listening, 30-minute global replay, headshots/channel notes,
and chat with images, reactions, and voice notes.

Source: [Shure WAVETOOL 4](https://www.shure.com/en-US/products/software/wavetool4)

Shure's theatre case study describes large casts changing costumes, wigs,
roles, and microphone positions; backstage mobile listening during quick
changes; and an explicit goal that backstage teams investigate independently so
the front-of-house engineer can remain focused on mixing. Its collaboration
examples attach messages to channels/timelines and use images, reactions, and
speech-to-text to reduce context switching.

Source: [How live theatre scales microphone monitoring](https://www.shure.com/en-ASIA/insights/wavetool-how-live-theater-scales-mic-monitoring-for-40-performers)

## Product conclusions

### A1 default

- protect attention needed for the console and artistic mix;
- show active/next sources, programme-path confidence, high-severity exceptions,
  system/clock health and the status/owner of backstage investigation;
- make “I heard something” capture fast through a source/cue/replay marker;
- allow direct listening/replay without exposing every pack workflow; and
- receive concise progress and resolution, not every telemetry fluctuation.

### A2 default

- organize by performer/role, current cue, quick change, zone, microphone path
  and physical task;
- expose detailed RF, audio, battery, placement, element/pack/spare and check
  state;
- enable rapid claim, investigate, message, image, replay, swap and verify
  workflows while moving backstage; and
- surface the next safe intervention opportunity before raw severity alone.

### Shared collaboration

- context must travel with a message: source, role/person, cue, incident and/or
  replay marker;
- chat acknowledgment is not incident ownership or resolution;
- urgent paging must be rate-limited and explicit;
- foreground mobile use favors large touch targets and short predefined actions;
- the same truth is rendered differently; A1/A2 views cannot fork identity or
  incident state; and
- crews overlap, so workspace selection and permission assignment remain
  separate concepts.
